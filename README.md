桔子数科事件整理/Juzi Digital Technology Incident

一、暴雷事件的开始

2026年5月至6月，中国互联网各大社交平台、投诉类平台开始大规模出现用户爆料。大量用户反馈，自己在桔子数科相关平台的网络借贷明明已经还款，但仍遭到资方强行划扣，且实时征信显示用户未还款。

目前互联网普遍了解的暴雷原因，是平台宣传并引导用户进行刷单，告知用户：

“短期免息借款，借完即还赚佣金，每单60-75元。”

用户在平台申请6000-8000元贷款，到账后转入桔子数科账户，代理随即发放佣金，APP同步显示账单结清。

不少人为了赚取佣金，可能刷了几十单。用户以为自己的每一笔借款均在第一时间全额还款，没有任何欠款。

但平台并未把这些资金给到实际出资的一众持牌消费金融及银行机构，反而对资金进行截留、挪用。而资方仍按照协议持续向借款人进行催收，并向征信系统报送逾期记录。

二、刷单还款可能只是冰山一角

以上是目前爆料出的信息。

用户一般只关心两个问题：

1. 自己的征信是否会恢复；
2. 借款合同是否能够确认已经履约。

然而，用户刷单涉及的金额可能只是冰山一角，很少有人继续深究其暴雷的根本原因，以及被截留的资金究竟去了哪里。

这背后其实是一场精心策划已久的庞氏骗局。

2025年10月新政之后，其大部分产品贷款年化利率下调至20%，但如此高的利率依旧亏损。

核心原因就是其用户群体风险过高，坏账率极高。贷款年化利率下调至20%后，已经不足以使其继续实现盈利。

银行放款后，这部分资金同样被挪用或截流到指定账户。

1. How the Crisis Began

Between May and June 2026, a large number of user reports began appearing across major Chinese social-media platforms and consumer-complaint websites.

Many users reported that loans they had taken out through platforms associated with Juzi Digital Technology (桔子数科) had already been repaid, yet the actual funding institutions continued to forcibly debit money from their accounts. At the same time, their real-time credit records continued to show that the loans remained unpaid.

Based on information currently circulating online, a widely discussed cause of the crisis was that the platforms had promoted and encouraged users to participate in commission-based transactions, telling them:

“Short-term interest-free loans. Borrow and repay immediately to earn a commission of RMB 60–75 per transaction.”

Users would apply for loans of approximately RMB 6,000–8,000 through the platform. After receiving the funds, they would transfer the money into accounts controlled or designated by Juzi Digital Technology. The agents would then immediately pay the users their commissions, while the app would simultaneously show that the corresponding loan had been fully settled.

Some users, seeking to earn more commissions, may have completed dozens of such transactions.

From the users’ perspective, every loan had been repaid immediately and in full, and they believed that they had no outstanding debt.

However, the platform did not transfer these funds to the licensed consumer-finance companies and banks that had actually funded the loans. Instead, the funds were withheld or misappropriated.

Meanwhile, the actual funding institutions continued collection activities in accordance with their loan agreements and reported the borrowers’ loans as overdue to China’s credit-reporting system.

2. The Commission-Based Transactions May Be Only the Tip of the Iceberg

The above represents the information that has currently been exposed through user reports and other disclosures.

Most affected users are primarily concerned about two questions:

1. Whether their personal credit records will eventually be restored or corrected;
2. Whether their loan contracts will be recognized as having been fully performed and repaid.

However, the money involved in these commission-based transactions may represent only the tip of the iceberg.

Very few people have investigated further into the fundamental cause of the company’s collapse or asked where the intercepted funds ultimately went.

Behind all of this was, in fact, a long-planned and carefully orchestrated Ponzi scheme.

4. From Juzi Fenqi to Juduoduo

For years, the company operated an online micro-lending business under the banner of being a loan-facilitation platform.

Its predecessor was Juzi Fenqi (桔子分期).

After China’s campus-loan sector experienced a major regulatory crackdown and Juzi Fenqi’s campus-lending business was halted by regulators, the business re-emerged in 2017 under the name Juduoduo (桔多多).

Before the new regulatory policy introduced in October 2025, the annualized interest rates on its loans had long remained at approximately 36%.

Its customer base consisted primarily of high-risk borrowers whom traditional banks were generally unwilling to serve.

Following the October 2025 regulatory changes, the annualized interest rates on most of its lending products were reduced to approximately 20%.

However, even at such a high interest rate, the business continued to suffer losses.

The fundamental reason was that its borrower population carried extremely high credit risk and consequently generated a very high bad-debt rate.

After annualized lending rates were reduced to approximately 20%, the resulting revenue was no longer sufficient for the company to remain profitable.

5. What Changed After the October 2025 Regulatory Policy

Following the new regulatory policy introduced in October 2025, a carefully designed variant of a Ponzi scheme began to unfold.

First, Juzi Digital Technology nominally separated and relocated Huiju Kehan (慧聚科瀚).

The actual control and lending entry points of multiple apps under the group were subsequently transferred or adjusted to Huiju Kehan.

Huiju Kehan’s core product, Yikoudai (宜口袋), secretly continued maintaining annualized lending rates of approximately 36% before the crisis erupted.

At the same time, two operations began to take place in parallel.

5.1 Encouraging Users to Perform Commission-Based Transactions and Intercepting Repayments

On one side was the mechanism described earlier:

Users were encouraged to repeatedly borrow and repay loans for commissions, while their repayment funds were intercepted.

Users believed that they had successfully repaid their loans, and the app itself displayed the corresponding bills as fully settled.

However, the licensed consumer-finance institutions and banks that had actually provided the loan capital did not receive the corresponding repayment funds.

5.2 Creating “Virtual Persons” to Obtain Loans

On the other side, Yikoudai, controlled by Huiju Kehan, began using existing data to create large numbers of fake borrowers — referred to internally or descriptively as “virtual persons” (虚拟人).

The identities of these “virtual persons” were then used to obtain loans through the platform.

After banks disbursed the loans, these funds were likewise misappropriated or redirected into designated accounts.

At this point, the necessary funds had been accumulated.

The next stage was the final phase of the scheme.

6. Overseas Lending Operations and Transfer of Funds

During 2025, Juzi Digital Technology secretly procured and deployed an overseas micro-lending service from an external provider, primarily targeting markets such as Mexico.

After operating the service for a period of time, it accumulated a certain amount of overseas user information.

By June 2026, Huiju Kehan’s Yikoudai had established its own overseas lending operation in Mexico.

At the same time, all user data collected through the previously outsourced overseas lending service was imported into Yikoudai’s overseas platform.

The same methods used previously were then applied again to create large numbers of overseas “virtual persons.”

These “virtual persons” were used to conduct massive volumes of borrowing and commission-based transactions through Yikoudai’s overseas lending platform.

Through this process, the funds were successfully transferred from China to overseas destinations.
